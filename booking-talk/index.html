Every AI Initiative Is an Experiment.
Nobody's Treating It Like One.

Bjarn Brunenberg · May 18th, 2026 · 15–20 min talk + Q&A
Topic: AI in Experimentation

Abstract

"We've spent eighteen months on AI. I couldn't tell you which pilots are working."

That's what a director of product at a large enterprise told me during one of a series of in-depth interviews I've been conducting with senior managers across Dutch and European organisations. Not the polished conference version of their AI story — the real one.

The patterns across these conversations are striking. Every single manager described top-down pressure to "do something with AI" — with no framework for what "something" means. Teams launch pilots that never reach production because nobody defined what success or failure looks like before they started. And when I asked "how do you decide whether to scale or kill an AI project?", almost nobody had an answer.

Here's the twist: this isn't an AI problem. It's an experimentation problem — one that's hiding in plain sight.

Every AI initiative inside these organisations is, by definition, an experiment. Unknown outcomes, uncertain ROI, high variability. Yet nobody is applying the discipline we use every day: hypotheses before tools, a single success metric before scale, and pre-defined kill criteria before kickoff. The result? Pilot purgatory — initiatives that launch, linger, and never ship.

In this talk, I'll share what I found across my interviews: the recurring patterns, the surprising blind spots, and the one discipline that separates organisations shipping AI from organisations stuck in limbo. Spoiler: it's the discipline this room already has.

You'll leave with a practical framework to turn any AI initiative into a structured, measurable experiment — complete with hypotheses, decision points, and a kill switch.

What the Research Shows

I've been interviewing senior managers and directors at organisations ranging from scale-ups to large enterprises — from media and e-commerce to travel and fashion. The goal: understand what's actually happening with AI adoption behind the LinkedIn posts.

Pattern 01

The Automation Paradox

People assume AI automates things out of the box. When they discover the setup work required, they disengage. As one AI lead put it: "The paradox in AI is that people think it automates stuff, but you have to do a lot of stuff to get stuff automated well." The expectation gap kills momentum before any real progress is made.

Pattern 02

Pilot Purgatory

Teams launch proof-of-concepts with no hypothesis, no single KPI, and no pre-defined decision point. The result: pilots that run indefinitely without anyone knowing whether they've succeeded or failed. Nobody asks "what would make us kill this?" before they start — so nothing ever gets killed, and nothing ever ships.

Pattern 03

The Translation Layer Gap

C-suite mandates "we need to do AI." Teams interpret this as "use ChatGPT more." The critical middle layer — someone who translates strategic intent into structured, measurable initiatives — is missing in almost every organisation. One director called himself "the translation layer" and said most companies don't have one. He's right.

Pattern 04

Tool-First Thinking

Organisations jump straight to AI tools without understanding the process they're trying to improve. The pattern is consistent: business outcome should come first, then the process, then the tools. Most people skip to step three. One consultant spent 50 questions defining a client's process before any AI was applied — the client got frustrated, but it was the only way to get real results.

Pattern 05

Training ≠ Adoption

Every organisation runs AI training sessions. Awareness goes up. Behaviour doesn't change. One company measured it precisely: awareness moved from 2.5 to 3.5 after three training sessions. But hands-on usage barely shifted. The only intervention that actually worked? Hackathons where people build something real in a constrained timeframe — with a problem they personally care about solving.

Pattern 06

The Benchmark Illusion

Every manager assumed their competitors were further ahead. When I cross-referenced the interviews, they're all clustered at the same early maturity stage. The relief was visible when they heard it. The race isn't as far along as anyone thinks — which means the window to build a real advantage is still wide open.

What the Audience Leaves With

  1. A clear picture of where organisations actually stand with AI — based on real interview data, not hype. Cross-referenced patterns that show where the industry is stuck and why.
  2. The "AI Experiment" framework — a practical method to turn any AI initiative into a structured experiment with a hypothesis, a single success metric, a timeframe, and a kill switch. Built on the same experimentation discipline this audience already practices.
  3. A provocation they can take back to their teams — the argument that AI adoption isn't a technology challenge, it's an experimentation maturity challenge. And that experimenters are uniquely positioned to lead it.

Talk Structure (15–20 min)

Opening hook · 2 min

A real quote from my research that reframes the room's assumptions. Create tension between what people assume is happening with AI and what's actually happening.

The research · 5 min

Walk through the key patterns from the interviews. Real quotes, real data, no sanitised corporate summaries. Let the audience recognise their own organisations in the findings.

The reframe · 3 min

The reveal: every single pattern maps to a known experimentation anti-pattern. Pilot purgatory = running tests without hypotheses. Tool-first thinking = skipping discovery. The translation layer gap = no experimentation programme owner. The audience already knows how to solve these — they just haven't been told to apply it to AI.

The framework · 5 min

Introduce the "AI Experiment" framework: how to take any AI initiative and structure it as a proper experiment. Hypothesis, single metric, timeframe, decision criteria. With a concrete before/after example from the interviews.

Close + call to action · 2 min

The window is still open. Organisations that apply experimentation discipline to AI now will pull ahead — not because they move faster, but because they learn faster. End with a direct challenge to the room.
